## TL;DR

Best idea today: **simple inventory and invoicing for tiny wholesale manufacturers**. It is boring, paid tools already exist, and the buyer has a clear daily pain.

The invoice chasing idea is also buildable, but it may be too easy for invoicing tools to add as a feature.

## Buildable Ideas

### Simple inventory and invoicing for tiny wholesale manufacturers

**Category:** Vertical SaaS

**Problem:** Tiny manufacturers need one simple way to track raw materials, finished goods, wholesale orders, and invoices.

**What's already out there:** InventoryQuick starts at $19/mo. Zoho Inventory has a free tier. Ordoro is listed at $59/mo. inFlow is around $89/mo. Cin7 is listed at $349/mo. Brightpearl is listed at $500/mo. Craftybase serves small makers, but pricing is unclear. Many paid tools already exist, which is a good sign.

**Who'd pay:** Owner-operators of very small wholesale manufacturers who make products and sell them to shops or other businesses.

**How you'd win:** Do not build a huge business system. Be the simple step between spreadsheets and costly tools. Focus on small batch makers who need customer price lists, batch builds, and fast setup.

**First build:** Build one flow: buy raw materials, make finished goods in batches, update stock, create a wholesale order, and send an invoice. Add CSV import, customer price lists, low-stock alerts, and QuickBooks export.

**Niche test:** Tiny wholesale makers using spreadsheets plus QuickBooks today. 3/5.

**Boring test:** This is stock, orders, bills, and invoices. Very unsexy daily work. 5/5.

**Big-company risk:** Medium. QuickBooks, Zoho, or Shopify-related inventory tools could add more of this.

**Confidence:** Medium. It could fail if buyers only need normal inventory plus invoices and do not care about the small manufacturing flow.

**Sources:** [Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/smallbusiness/comments/4qldcs/wholesale_inventoryinvoice_software_suggestion)

### Automatic invoice chasing for freelancers

**Category:** Fintech

**Problem:** Freelancers lose time and cash because clients pay late and they hate chasing by hand.

**What's already out there:** Invoice Ninja talks about late freelancer invoices, pricing unclear. Remitly writes about late invoices hurting cash flow, pricing unclear. Bluevine says nearly 3 in 10 small business owners delay paying themselves because invoices are delayed, pricing unclear.

**Who'd pay:** Solo freelancers and very small service businesses that send invoices and chase payment themselves.

**How you'd win:** Do one narrow job better than normal invoicing tools: send calm, professional follow-ups and give clients an easy way to pay or explain a dispute.

**First build:** Build an email-only tool. The user adds client name, invoice amount, due date, and email. The tool sends polite reminders before and after the due date, shows overdue invoices, and gives the client one page to pay or respond.

**Niche test:** Solo freelancers with slow-paying business clients, especially designers, writers, consultants, and developers. 3/5.

**Boring test:** This is payment follow-up and admin work. Boring, but a bit closer to a feature than a full business. 4/5.

**Big-company risk:** Medium. QuickBooks, FreshBooks, Stripe, PayPal, or Invoice Ninja could add stronger reminder flows.

**Confidence:** Medium. It could fail because users may see this as a small reminder feature inside their invoicing tool, not a separate product.

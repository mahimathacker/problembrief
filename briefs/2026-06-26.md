## TL;DR

Developers are frustrated with VSCode's limited error/warning visibility, as it only highlights issues in open files, not across all project files. There's a clear call for improved tooling to navigate errors effectively. Additionally, managing Terraform's destruction of resources remains an ongoing headache.

## Opportunities

### VSCode's Lack of Bulk Error/Warning Visibility

**The Problem:** Developers juggle terminal workarounds to locate and correct errors because VSCode doesn't display issues across all files within a project, just the ones currently open.

**Why Now:** As project complexity and file count grow, piecemeal error checking becomes inefficient and error-prone, demanding a more consolidated approach.

**Who'd Pay:** Dev teams hungry for productivity boosts and error mitigation will pay for a solution that integrates seamlessly with their existing workflows.

**Buildability:** High potential—integrate with existing APIs and improve on existing extension capabilities.

**Score:** pain 4 · freq 5 · build 4 · market 4 · interest 5  
**Sources:** [GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/13953)

### Inability to Exclude Specific Resources During Terraform Destroy

**The Problem:** There's a persistent risk of destroying essential resources like RDS when running Terraform destroy commands, with no elegant way to exclude them.

**Why Now:** As infrastructure as code becomes the norm, ensuring resource safety in automated processes is not just desirable—it's crucial.

**Who'd Pay:** Infrastructure teams and DevOps engineers looking to safeguard key infrastructure components would pay to avoid costly downtime.

**Buildability:** Moderate—requires deep understanding of Terraform's operation and potential engagement with the community or HashiCorp.

**Score:** pain 4 · freq 4 · build 4 · market 4 · interest 5  
**Sources:** [GitHub](https://github.com/hashicorp/terraform/issues/2253)

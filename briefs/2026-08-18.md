## TL;DR
Today's strongest idea is "Open Media Sync for Trakt Free Users." Many people used Trakt.tv's free service to sync their media watch history. Now, Trakt.tv charges for this. There's a chance to build a simple, low-cost tool to fill this gap.

## Buildable Ideas

### Open Media Sync for Trakt Free Users
**Category:** Devtools
**Problem:** Trakt.tv now charges for its API, stopping free users from syncing their media watch history with other apps.
**What's already out there:** Trakt.tv (now charges for API access), plextraktsync (broken for free users). Prices are unclear. Many tools here is a good sign; it means people want this kind of service.
**Who'd pay:** People who love media and use apps like Plex or Kodi. They used to rely on Trakt.tv's free service to keep their watch history in sync.
**How you'd win:** Offer a free or cheap service that does the main thing Trakt.tv now charges VIP users for: syncing media watch history.
**First build:** A simple website. Users connect their media apps (like Plex, Kodi). This site tracks what they watch and syncs it, with a basic way for other apps to connect.
**Niche test:** This is for media fans who use multiple apps and want their watch history to match everywhere. Niche score: 4/5.
**Boring test:** This is a useful, behind-the-scenes tool that keeps data in sync. It's not flashy, but it solves a real problem. Boring score: 4/5.
**Big-company risk:** medium. Plex or Kodi could build this themselves, or a new media tracking company could buy it.
**Confidence:** medium. The biggest risk is that not enough people are affected, or they just pay Trakt VIP instead.

## Research Leads

### Secure Local Certificate Access for Self-Hosted Apps
**Category:** Small business
**Problem:** Self-hosted apps can't easily use local security certificates for signing documents. This forces users to upload private keys, which is risky.
**What's already out there:** Stirling PDF (mentioned as having the problem). No clear competitor yet.
**Who'd pay:** People using their own document signing apps. They need to use local security certificates (like from Windows) without sending their private keys to the server.
**How you'd win:** Create a secure local program. It would let self-hosted apps use local certificates for signing. The private key would never leave the user's computer.
**First build:** A small program on the user's computer. It would have a simple way for the self-hosted server to ask it to sign documents. It would use the local certificate and send back the signed document.
**Niche test:** This is for users of specific self-hosted document signing tools who care deeply about private key security. Niche score: 4/5.
**Boring test:** This is a technical security tool for a specific workflow. It's very much unsexy, operational work. Boring score: 4/5.
**Big-company risk:** medium. Document management companies or cloud signing services could build this.
**Confidence:** low. We don't know if enough people have this exact problem, or if the existing apps could just add this feature themselves.
**Sources:**
* [GitHub](https://github.com/Stirling-Tools/Stirling-PDF/issues/7316)

### Non-Habit Chronic Illness Tracker
**Category:** Health
**Problem:** People with long-term illnesses struggle to track their symptoms regularly. They often stop using tracking tools quickly.
**What's already out there:** Apps, Paper journals, Google Sheets, Notes. Prices are unclear.
**Who'd pay:** People living with chronic illnesses.
**How you'd win:** Make a tracking tool that doesn't need daily effort. It would be easy for people with chronic illnesses to keep records without building a habit.
**First build:** A simple tool that asks for updates at random times. Or, it could use very quick ways to input info, like voice or one tap, to make it less work.
**Niche test:** This is for people with chronic illnesses who have tried and failed to use habit-based trackers. Niche score: 3/5.
**Boring test:** This is about making a personal health tool easier to use. It's not exciting, but it helps with a daily struggle. Boring score: 4/5.
**Big-company risk:** medium. Large health tech companies or existing illness management platforms could offer this.
**Confidence:** low. We need to find out if many people share this specific problem and would pay for a solution. Also, making a truly "non-habit" tracker that still works well is very hard.
